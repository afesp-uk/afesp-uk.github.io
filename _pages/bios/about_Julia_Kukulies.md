I am an AFESP Research Fellow at the University of Reading, where I lead the project [Resolving Rain]({% link _projects/Resolving_rain_project.md %}). In this project, I investigate the **benefits of global km-scale models for forecasting impactful precipitation events**, and how we can use **satellite observations to quantify these benefits**. By studying precipitation processes that emerge at the km-scale, I hope to provide clearer evidence on when and why explicitly resolved convection improves forecasts and where it doesn't. The focus is on recent experiments in global km-scale forecasting, including, for example, ECMWF’s [Weather-Induced Extremes Digital Twin](https://destine.ecmwf.int/weather-induced-extremes-digital-twin/) and forecasts conducted by the UK Met Office.

I have a PhD from the University of Gothenburg and before joining the University of Reading in May 2026, I was a postdoctoral fellow in the Advanced Study Program at the [National Center for Atmospheric Research](https://ncar.ucar.edu/) in Boulder, Colorado (2023–2026). During my time at NCAR, I developed a framework to analyze precipitation efficiency in severe convection, as one aspect that influences how much precipitation we get at the surface, where it ultimately influences us. I am planning to apply this framework as part of my AFESP project to test if a better representation of precipitation efficiency contributes to better forecast of impactful precipitation events.
