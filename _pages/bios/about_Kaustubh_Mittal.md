<p style="text-align: justify;">
I am a fourth-year PhD researcher in the Department of Meteorology at the University of Reading. Through my PhD, I investigate the dynamics, predictability and machine-learning based prediction of medium-range European forecast busts in operational numerical weather prediction systems. My research combines atmospheric dynamics, forecast verification and explainable artificial intelligence techniques to better understand why forecast busts occur and whether they can be anticipated from large-scale atmospheric initial conditions. I am supervised by Robert Plant (University of Reading), Suzanne Gray (University of Reading), David Flack (Met Office) and Dave Parsons (NorthWest Research Associates, Boulder, CO).
<p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
I hold a Bachelor's degree in Mechanical Engineering from the National Institute of Technology, Jalandhar, and a Master's degree in Ocean Engineering and Naval Architecture from the Indian Institute of Technology Kharagpur. During my Master's research on Marine Heatwaves in the Northern Indian Ocean under the supervision of Prof. Prasad K. Bhaskaran, I developed a strong interest in geophysical fluid dynamics and climate variability, which motivated my subsequent transition to atmospheric science and numerical weather prediction.
<p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
My research interests include numerical weather prediction, atmospheric dynamics, numerical modelling,  computational mathematics and machine learning for weather and climate applications.
