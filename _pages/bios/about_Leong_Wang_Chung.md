<p style="text-align: justify;">
Hi, I am a first-year PhD student in the Department of Meteorology at the University of Reading. I am currently working on the project “Improving subseasonal-to-seasonal forecasts in the tropics using AI”.
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
In recent years, many AI-based weather prediction (AIWP) models have been developed, including AIFS, GraphCast and FourCastNet. These models can produce medium-range forecasts with skill comparable to traditional numerical weather prediction (NWP) models, while requiring substantially less computational resources. However, the performance of different types of AIWP models at subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) lead times remains largely unassessed. In this project, I will investigate the strengths and weaknesses of various types of AIWP models. I will then explore methods for combining their outputs to improve the accuracy and reliability of tropical S2S forecasts.
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
My supervisors are <a href="#kieran-hunt">Kieran Hunt</a> (NCAS), Steve Woolnough (NCAS) and Steffen Tietsche (ECMWF).
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Before starting my PhD, I obtained my BSc in Physics and Earth System Science from the Chinese University of Hong Kong. My capstone project involved using deep learning approaches to downscale atmospheric variables for climate risk assessment. During my undergraduate studies, I also undertook a one-year placement at the Hong Kong Observatory, where I worked on projects related to NWP post-processing using deep learning methods.
</p>
