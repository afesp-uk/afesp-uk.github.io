I am an AFESP Doctoral Researcher at the University of Reading (Department of Meteorology), working on machine learning-driven background error covariances for sub-kilometre data assimilation. My research is supported by the AFESP-DTP with additional funding and collaboration through the Met Office CASE Award and the National Centre for Earth Observation (<a href="https://www.nceo.ac.uk/">NCEO</a>). My lead supervisor is <a href="#ross-bannister">Dr Ross Bannister</a> (University of Reading & NCEO), alongside co-supervisors <a href="#amos-lawless">Prof. Amos Lawless</a> (University of Reading & NCEO), Dr Christopher Thomas (Met Office@Reading), and Dr Hong Wei (University of Reading, Computer Science).
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
As numerical weather prediction models move towards sub-kilometre grid scales (100–300 m), convection and sharp boundary-layer inversions become explicitly resolved, resulting in forecast errors that are highly localised, anisotropic, and strongly flow-dependent. Standard operational variational data assimilation (VarDA) models the background error covariance matrix <b>B</b> using a control-variable transform (<b>B</b> = <b>UU</b><sup>T</sup>, <b>U</b> = <b>U</b><sub>p</sub><b>U</b><sub>h</sub><b>U</b><sub>v</sub>), where a static, climatological vertical transform <b>U</b><sub>v</sub> risks unphysically spreading increments across features like capping inversions. However, computing flow-dependent vertical transforms via an Ensemble of Data Assimilations (EDA) at sub-km scale is computationally prohibitive for operational forecasting time windows. My PhD investigates whether machine learning can estimate ensemble-quality, flow-dependent vertical error covariances from a single deterministic forecast without running an operational ensemble.
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Before starting my PhD, I completed a Bachelor's in Computational Physics, followed by an MSc in Meteorology focusing on deep learning methods to detect cloud types from geostationary satellite imagery. I then completed an MTech in Remote Sensing & GIS (specialising in Marine and Atmospheric Sciences), where I worked on hyperspectral satellite data assimilation for regional weather prediction and tropical cyclone track and intensity forecasting. Across my research journey, the unifying thread has been bridging physics, satellite remote sensing, data assimilation, and machine learning.
