<p style="text-align: justify;">
Ted is a specialist in large-scale atmospheric dynamics and circulation and its role in climate variability and change, including extreme events. He was originally trained in Math &amp; Physics and Geophysical Fluid Dynamics, and spent 24 years on faculty at the University of Toronto before moving to Reading in 2012. Ted has held leadership roles in international scientific assessments of climate (IPCC) and stratospheric ozone (WMO/UNEP), and in the World Climate Research Programme (WCRP); was Chief Editor of the Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences (the premiere journal in fundamental aspects of atmospheric science) from 2001-2005; co-authored the US National Academy of Sciences report on Extreme Weather Events and Climate Change Attribution (2016); and chaired the Science Review Group of the Met Office Hadley Centre (2017-2021). He was elected Fellow of the Royal Society in 2016. Ted’s recent research has pioneered a ‘storyline’ approach to representing the deep uncertainty in aspects of climate variability and change related to atmospheric circulation, including extreme events. This has motivated him to question much of the current statistical practice within climate science and to begin engaging with stakeholders (e.g. on drought risk), and in inter-disciplinary collaborations (with philosophers of science, psychologists, and an anthropologist). He currently co-chairs the WCRP’s Lighthouse Activity ‘My Climate Risk’.
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Ted’s interest in the AFESP programme lies mainly in the S2S prediction theme, where model deficiencies, limited observational constraints and statistical non-stationarity challenge traditional statistical approaches and call for novel, physically informed and interpretable representations of uncertainty and risk.
</p>
