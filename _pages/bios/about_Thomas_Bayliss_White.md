I'm a PhD student that has been working in the Department of Geography and Environmental Science under the supervision of Shovonlal Roy (University of Reading) and David Ford (Met Office) since September 2025.
My research focuses on how data assimilation is used to improve forecasts and reanalysis datasets of ocean biogeochemistry, which includes variables such as plankton and nutrient concentrations.
I work with the Met Office's Forecast Ocean Assimilation Model (FOAM), coupling the MEDUSA biogeochemical model to NEMO ocean physics and using NEMOVAR to assimilate satellite observations of phytoplankton chlorophyll and carbon.
By improving the quality of these forecasts and reanalyses, scientists and governments are better able to monitor the past and current state of the health of our oceans, identify how best to take action to prevent ecological and economic damage and meet environmental reporting obligations.

Some of the topics I plan on researching include:

- Investigating whether satellite observations of both phytoplankton chlorophyll and carbon can effectively constrain forecasts of nutrient concentration.
- Using ensemble data assimilation to estimate how biogeochemical parameter values vary seasonally and across different regions of the ocean.
- Differentiating between harmful and non-harmful species of phytoplankton, and seeing whether data assimilation can improve forecasts of the concentration of toxins that harmful species produce.

From June-September 2023, I worked as a research intern at Mount Royal University in Calgary, AB, Canada through the Mitacs Globalink research internship scheme, studying data assimilation applied to epidemiological models. 
Through these experiences, I have developed an interest in working at the interface of applied mathematics, meteorology and ecology, which drew me to apply to the AFESP DTP.
