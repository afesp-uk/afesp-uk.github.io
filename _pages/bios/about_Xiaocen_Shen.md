<p style="text-align: justify;">
I’m Xiaocen Shen, an Independent Research Fellow in the Department of Meteorology at the University of Reading, supported by a Leverhulme Early Career Fellowship.
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
My research focuses on large-scale atmospheric dynamics, particularly stratosphere–troposphere coupling and its role in climate variability and predictability. I take a forensic approach to understanding climate variability, combining statistical and dynamical perspectives to investigate how atmospheric teleconnections vary across timescales, including their nonstationarity and state dependence. More recently, I have also become interested in exploring what AI/ML can tell us about atmospheric dynamics and predictability.
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Beyond my research, I am keen to see early career researchers have a stronger voice and play a greater role in shaping the meteorological community. I also serve on the Council of the Royal Meteorological Society.
</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">
Within AFESP, I serve as a PhD supervisor for <a href="#andrew-kenny">Andrew Kenny</a>, whose project investigates the connection between the late-spring stratospheric polar vortex and the subseasonal-to-seasonal predictability of the summer North Atlantic Oscillation.
</p>
