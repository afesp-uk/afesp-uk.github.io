**Supervisor**: Xiaocen Shen

**PhD Student**: Andrew Kenny

**Description**: The Summer North Atlantic Oscillation (SNAO) is the primary mode of climate variability across the summer north Atlantic region. The phase of the SNAO affects the north Atlantic storm track, and thus temperature and precipitation across much of Europe, making forecasting the SNAO important. Despite the importance of accurately forecasting the SNAO, current forecasting systems have little skill in forecasting the SNAO, especially in comparison to its winter counterpart. Much of this lack of skill can be attributed to lessened variability across most climate modes, and the collapse of the winter stratospheric polar vortex, thought to lessen the opportunity for troposphere-stratosphere coupling. However, the paradigm of a passive summer stratosphere has been challenged in recent years, with a significant correlation between the May polar vortex and SNAO identified.

This project investigates the potential link between the strength of the Stratospheric polar vortex in the late spring and the SNAO. The goal is to diagnose the relationship between the late spring polar vortex and SNAO using reanalysis products and large ensembles, evaluate how well this relationship is captured in S2S forecast models, and to conduct targeted experiments on forecast models to identify ways to improve SNAO prediction.
