**Supervisor**: Kieran Hunt

**PhD Student**: Leong Wang Chung

**Description**: Subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasts are important for decision-making in agriculture, health, energy and water security across the tropics. However, current operational forecasts rely largely on physics-based numerical weather prediction models and often show limited or variable skill in tropical regions. Recent AI-based weather prediction models can produce forecasts of large-scale atmospheric fields with skill comparable to traditional models, but at substantially lower computational cost, creating new opportunities for their use within ensemble forecasting systems.

This project investigates the strengths and weaknesses of different approaches to AI weather prediction at S2S lead times, including standard AI models, models trained specifically for S2S prediction, and hybrid AI–physics models. It will assess how these models compare with traditional numerical weather prediction systems in the tropics, and explore how they can best be combined within existing ensembles, with the aim of improving the accuracy and reliability of tropical S2S forecasts.
