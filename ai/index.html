AI in weather and climate science

Machine learning is changing how we observe, model and predict the Earth system. This page collects upcoming and recent workshops, conferences and news on AI for weather and climate, and the AFESP projects working in this area.

      Duncan Watson-Parris and colleagues will use AI to improve climate models and guide new ocean observations, including adapted Argo floats, through the AI for Science Impact Challenge.

    • 24 September 2026 AI dominates the conversation at Climate Week NYC MIT Technology Review

      AI, both as a climate tool and as a growing source of energy demand, was the main theme of this year's Climate Week in New York.

    • 11 August 2026AFESP Droughts unlikely to ease soon, AI models show University of Reading

      AFESP PhD researcher Karan Ruparell, who builds AI river-flow forecasting models, comments on England's drought outlook.

    • 8 July 2026 AI can produce trustworthy, physically realistic weather forecasts Met Office

      The Met Office and the Alan Turing Institute's FastNet model, trained with physical principles, now performs comparably to the Met Office Global Model and better captures extreme storms.

    • 12 May 2026 ECMWF's AIFS v2 goes live ECMWF

      The machine-learned AIFS Single and Ensemble move to version 2, adding a wave component and ECMWF's first data-driven snow cover forecasts.
